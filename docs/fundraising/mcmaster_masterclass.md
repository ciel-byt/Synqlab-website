## What type of innovation are you developing?

**SynqLab is an agentic AI operating system for biological discovery — built for everyone from a small research group to an enterprise pharma R&D organization.**

Software has macOS. AI has PyTorch. Biology has… a folder of disconnected tools and a handful of specialists who know how it all fits together. We're building the OS that biology never had — a team of specialist AI agents that acts as the scientist's own research team. The scientist describes *what* they want in plain English; the agents handle *how* — picking the right tools, running them in the right order, writing their own code when needed, and learning from every result so the system gets sharper every day.

It's an AI software platform. But the deeper bet is that the next generation of medicine doesn't come from a new molecule — it comes from a new kind of *colleague*: an AI system that takes any scientist who can describe a hypothesis and gives them the productivity of a full computational team.

## Describe the problem that you are addressing

**Modern biology has too many tools, too little intelligence, and no system deciding what to try next.**

The same problem shows up across the entire discovery ecosystem: a scientist trying to design a new peptide therapeutic, antibody, or biosensor has to be three people at once — a biologist who understands the science, a software engineer who can wire up GPU clusters and reformat files between a dozen incompatible tools, and a data scientist who can train models on whatever data the team happens to have. Most of their week is spent on the parts that have nothing to do with biology.

It gets worse:

- **Failures get thrown away.** The candidates that *didn't* work are the most informative training data in the building — and they end up in a deleted folder.
- **Knowledge dies in notebooks and slide decks.** When a key scientist leaves — whether they're a graduate trainee or a 15-year R&D veteran — the team's hard-won intuition walks out the door with them.
- **Generic tools don't predict what you actually care about.** A standard score tells you whether a protein folds — not whether it will fluoresce, bind its target, or be safe as a drug.
- **Every new project starts from zero.** No memory. No transfer. The same mistakes get re-discovered round after round.

The bottleneck on modern medicine isn't the science. **It's everything around the science.** Every new therapeutic, diagnostic, or biosensor takes years and millions of dollars longer than it should — because the scientist has to do the work of an entire engineering and ML team before they get to do any biology.

## Describe your solution to the identified problem

**An agentic AI system that lets the scientist stay a scientist.**

A researcher opens SynqLab and says, in plain English: *"design peptide variants that are brighter, more stable, and don't aggregate."* Behind that one sentence, a team of specialist AI agents — coordinator and workers — gets to work. They pick the right computational tools (folding, sequence generation, docking, stability), run them in the right order, write their own analysis code on the fly when an existing tool doesn't fit, pull in relevant published research, and score every candidate. The scientist never sees a config file, a GPU job, or a file format conversion. They see results, reasoning, and a chat interface.

Three things make this not just another tool:

1. **It learns from failure.** Every result the system produces — the bright variants *and* the dim ones, the stable folds *and* the aggregating ones — feeds back into a lightweight model trained on the lab's own data. Round 2 is measurably better than round 1. Round 10 is a different planet.
2. **It tunes itself to each lab over time.** A team optimizing fluorescence gets a brightness model. A team optimizing enzyme activity gets an activity model. The system *specializes* into the lab's specific science as it accumulates results — knowledge stops dying in lab notebooks and starts compounding.
3. **The scientist stays in control.** Three modes — full copilot (every step approved), supervised (review at checkpoints), or fully autonomous (overnight runs). The same system serves a cautious regulatory-aware pharma R&D group and an impatient academic researcher who just wants results by morning.

Our first beachhead is peptide brightness in a split-GFP assay: fast, quantitative, and ideal for showing the platform's closed-loop learning in action. From there, the same operating system expands to peptide therapeutics, antibody design, enzyme engineering, and diagnostic biosensors. Once connected to physical lab equipment, SynqLab runs the entire design–build–test–learn cycle autonomously, overnight.

**Imagine one scientist — anywhere from an academic lab to an enterprise pharma R&D site — running a discovery program that today takes a team of ten.** That's what SynqLab unlocks.

## Who is your target market?

**Anyone running iterative molecular design** — from a small academic team to a 50-person pharma discovery group. We win them in three concentric rings:

1. **Beachhead — academic biomedical research groups** at research-intensive universities. Fast adoption, builds the data flywheel, generates publishable validation that de-risks the platform for larger customers.
2. **Mid-market — biotech R&D teams and contract research organizations** (5–50 scientists) that can't afford to build an in-house ML platform but can't compete without one.
3. **Enterprise — pharma R&D, large biotech, and diagnostics companies** — sold as on-premise / private cloud to satisfy data sovereignty, audit trails, and GxP compliance. **This is where the real money is**, but the academic + biotech traction is what gets us in the door.

- **Buyer** — Research grant or departmental budget (academic); R&D operations or digital R&D budget (industry).

In academic and small-biotech settings the Influencer and the User are usually the same person — which is why our go-to-market starts bottom-up there. In pharma we land the same way (a single team running a pilot) and then expand horizontally and upward into an enterprise contract.
